Abstract

Simulation-based data generation is essential for training and evaluating equirectangular panoramic (ERP) depth estimation models, where dense real-world ground truth (GT) is difficult to obtain. However, existing resources are either fixed pre-generated panoramic datasets or flexible generators designed mainly for perspective cameras, and therefore do not simultaneously provide panoramic output, scene-level flexibility, and geometrically consistent multimodal ground truth. We propose Urban360, a plug-and-play Unreal Engine 5 (UE5) plugin pipeline that generates panoramic synthetic data (RGB, Depth, Normal, and Camera Pose) for arbitrary UE5 scenes under a specified coordinate convention with pixel-wise consistency, together with the resulting Urban360 Dataset (3 scenes, 10,080 views). The pipeline renders RGB with Lumen global illumination (hardware ray tracing) and captures Depth from the geometry buffer for dense GT that sparse real-world LiDAR cannot provide, and stitches a low-distortion ERP via a 168-slice Geometric Look-Up Table (GeoLUT); Surface Normal is derived from GT Depth at the capture resolution, keeping all image modalities pixel-wise consistent in a unified FP16 EXR format. To validate the pipeline output, we use real-world outdoor panoramic depth estimation as a downstream task: fine-tuning three representative models (DA360, DAP, DA²) on Urban360 yields consistent improvements on the Helvipad outdoor benchmark, and under a matched protocol it achieves better overall transfer performance than the competing synthetic dataset PanoCity. The Urban360 plugin and dataset will be publicly released upon publication.

What is the Urban360 Plugin?

Unlike fixed synthetic datasets (e.g., pre-rendered image archives), Urban360 is provided as a drag-and-drop Unreal Engine 5 plugin. It empowers researchers to generate their own custom panoramic data from any UE5 environment to match their target domain.

  • Drag-and-Drop Setup: Simply place the CapturePoint actor into your scene.
  • Automated Capture: The plugin automatically captures 168 narrow-FOV slices using hardware-accelerated Lumen global illumination.
  • Seamless Export: It stitches the slices via a precomputed GeoLUT and exports perfectly aligned RGB, continuous Depth (meters), and Surface Normal as three separate FP16 EXR files (Color, Depth, Normal) plus a camera-pose manifest CSV.
  • Binary Release: The plugin ships compiled for Unreal Engine 5.7 and installs into any project, Blueprint-only ones included, with no build step. Its source code is not published.

The Dataset Generator panel inside the Unreal Editor. Capture points sit along a path in the level, and the Capture tab selects the stereo mode, the modalities to write, the capture count, the camera height and the local step.

Key Insights & Contributions

Dense GT Real Sensors Cannot Capture: Rotating LiDAR on benchmarks like Helvipad provides valid labels for only about 12% of ERP pixels (raised to 61% by depth completion). Physically based rendering (Lumen) yields dense GT even on glass, reflective, and low-light regions.

Plug-and-Play Advantage: Since Urban360 is a plug-and-play plugin for arbitrary UE5 scenes, it allows generating data tailored to the target domain, overcoming the negative transfer often caused by fixed synthetic datasets like PanoCity.
  • (i) A plug-and-play UE5 plugin pipeline that generates panoramic (ERP) synthetic data for arbitrary UE5 scenes; to our knowledge the first to jointly export RGB, Depth, Normal, and Camera Pose under a specified coordinate convention with pixel-wise consistency, using Lumen global illumination (hardware ray tracing) and 168-slice GeoLUT ERP stitching.
  • (ii) The Urban360 Dataset (3 scenes, 10,080 views) in a unified FP16 EXR format, with Surface Normal derived from GT Depth at the capture resolution (Eq. 1) for pixel-wise consistency.
  • (iii) Using real-world outdoor panoramic depth estimation as a downstream task, fine-tuning three representative models (DA360, DAP, DA²) on Urban360 gives consistent improvements on Helvipad outdoor (AbsRel −9 to −33%, δ1 +12 to +109%) and better overall transfer than the competing synthetic dataset PanoCity under a matched protocol.

Method: Capture Pipeline

The Urban360 capture pipeline: (a) anchor sampling along a road-spline path in a UE5 scene, (b) 168 narrow-FOV slices (24×7, 30°×30°) captured via SceneCapture with Lumen ray tracing, (c) GeoLUT equirectangular stitching, and (d) FP16 EXR output (RGB, Depth, Normal) with camera pose.

168-slice GeoLUT stitching: The slice grid (24 horizontal × 7 vertical, narrow FOV 30°×30°) is combined into an ERP via a GeoLUT precomputed for the configured output resolution, reducing edge distortion. The output resolution is a capture parameter rather than a fixed property of the pipeline: the panorama width W is configurable from 512 to 4096, and the slice render resolution scales with it, so the oversampling ratio is preserved at any setting. Each view is stereo-packed into a single W × W image (left eye on top, right eye on bottom, W × W/2 per eye). The experiments reported in this paper were run at W = 1920; the released Urban360 Dataset is captured at W = 1024.

Anti-ODS stereo and forced horizontality: Fixed baseline of ±3.2 cm per eye (6.4 cm total) with in-place rotation rather than translation preserves cross-slice geometric consistency. When placing CapturePoints, only the yaw component of the terrain normal is kept (pitch/roll=0) to prevent horizon distortion.

Output Modalities: RGB (LDR, ACES tonemapping, linear light), Depth (radial ray distance, meters), Camera Pose (world coords + quaternion).
Surface Normal: Derived from the same panoramic Depth (SCS_SceneDepth, R32F) via numerical differentiation. For ERP pixel (u,v), the surface coordinate is P(u,v) = D(u,v)·r̂(u,v), and Normal is the cross product of the two parametric tangents:

Because Depth and Normal are derived from the same source, Depth-Normal inconsistencies from independent stitching do not arise in principle. Stored in FP16 EXR files.

Screen-space reflection (top) versus Lumen front-layer tracing (middle) on a water surface. The two crops look identical, but in the Fourier spectrum of the water the peak at the slice frequency (period = W/24) falls to 0.64× of the screen-space level: a traced reflection no longer depends on which slice the pixel came from.

Urban360 Dataset & Comparisons

The released instance comprises 10,080 views collected across 3 static UE5 scenes, one view per anchor: each anchor was captured in single-shot mode, at its center position only. Every view is stored at W = 1024, a 1024×1024 file holding two 1024×512 equirectangular eyes. The plugin also supports a nine-node cluster per anchor, which this release does not use.

  • CitySample (49.1%, 4,952 views): Dense high-rise environment with glass facades and long-range vanishing points.
  • CityPark (42.9%, 4,325 views): Vegetation and irregular natural objects with transmissive/semi-transmissive materials.
  • DownTownWest (8.0%, 803 views): Dense urban grid of near- to mid-range commercial buildings.
Note: the released Urban360 dataset was re-captured after the paper's experiments and is not meant to reproduce the paper's fine-tuning numbers, which came from an earlier internal capture. To benchmark on this release, fine-tune and produce your own baseline from it.

Experiment Results

Fine-tuning three representative foundation models (DA360, DAP, DA²) on Urban360 yields consistent improvements. The magnitude of improvement is inversely related to base model strength. Against a comparable synthetic dataset (PanoCity), Urban360 shows better overall transfer performance, highlighting the value of plug-and-play domain matching versus fixed near-range biased datasets.

Supplementary: Geometric Validation

To directly validate the "FP16 geometric GT" claim independently of downstream models, we perform stereo reprojection sweeping a global depth scale s. If the exported depth correctly represents absolute metric distance, reprojection error must be minimized exactly at s = 1.0. Across 5,128 views (CityPark + DownTownWest), the optimal scale strictly aligns at 1.00 (median argmin 1.01).
